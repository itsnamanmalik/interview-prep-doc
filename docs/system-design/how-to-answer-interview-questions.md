# How to answer Interview Questions

<a class="watch-video" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i7twT3x5yv8">&#9654;&nbsp;Watch Video</a>

### Steps to Answer System Design Question:

❓ **Step 1**: Understand the problem and define the scope through clarifying questions.

📊 **Step 2**: Propose a high-level design and get agreement on APIs and architecture.

🔍 **Step 3**: Deep dive into key design areas to address potential challenges and trade-offs.

📋 **Step 4**: Wrap up by summarizing unique aspects of the design and leaving time for questions.

⏱️ **Time Allocation**: Suggested interview time breakdown for each step to optimize discussion.

### Key Insights:

- 🧠 **Structured Interviews Lead to Success:**

    > A framework reduces interview anxiety and allows candidates to showcase their skills effectively. This structured approach ensures that both candidates and interviewers can navigate complex topics efficiently.

- 🔑 **Clarifying Requirements is Crucial**:

    > Asking the right questions in the first step helps in identifying key features and non-functional requirements, which are essential for creating a robust design. This sets a strong foundation for the entire discussion.

- 📐 **APIs as Contracts**:

    > Establishing clear APIs early in the design process helps frame functional requirements and ensures a mutual understanding of the system's capabilities. This clarity prevents scope creep and miscommunication.

- ⚖️ **Trade-offs Matter**:

    > Diving deep into design challenges allows candidates to demonstrate problem-solving skills and the ability to weigh different solutions. Discussing trade-offs showcases a mature understanding of system design complexities.

- 📈 **Non-Functional Requirements are Key**:

    > As roles become more senior, the emphasis on scalability and performance increases. Understanding these requirements is vital for creating systems that can handle real-world demands.

- 💡 **Iterative Design Process**:

    > Should revisit and refine their designs as the interview progresses, which is crucial for identifying potential pitfalls early on and adjusting accordingly.
